Guide 08

How to read your report

Generate a report from the portal whenever you want one, and you get a short email and a longer PDF. This is a walk-through of both: what each number means, how to read the trend, and what to actually do about it. No CIS jargon without an explanation attached.

The two documents

The email is a 150–200 word summary: your score, the three things worth acting on right now, and a link to the full PDF. It's written for whoever makes the call on your estate (owner, office manager, whoever), not for whoever fixes it.

The PDF is the detail: every open finding, every CVE, and a per-device breakdown. It's the thing to forward to your IT contact or keep on file for an auditor.

The headline number

The number at the top is your CIS compliance score: the share of CIS Level 1 controls (encryption, firewall, screen lock, and the rest of the baseline) configured correctly across your estate right now. It's a percentage out of 100. Higher is better.

SignalWhat it shows
CIS compliance scoreThe share of CIS Level 1 controls passing across your devices. This is the headline number.
CVE correlationsPotential exposures linked to observed software identity and version evidence, with severity and confidence context.
OS currencyHow many devices report current, pending or stale operating-system update evidence.

These stay separate rather than being blended into one figure, so a clean CVE count can't quietly cover for a weak CIS score, or the other way round. Each number tells you something different, and you see all of them together, not averaged away.

The point of the compliance score is to give you a single thing to watch improve month to month. It isn't a pass/fail grade; it's a direction of travel.

The trend

Next to the compliance score you'll see a change from the previous week, shown as +7 or −4. A drop is always framed as what's left to recover, not as a verdict: the point is to know where you stand, not to feel bad about it. The PDF adds a longer view of the same trend so you can see whether things are heading the right way over a longer stretch, not just since last week.

Your first report is different. With no history yet, there's no trend to show. Instead you'll see “baseline established”: that first report is the complete picture of your estate today, and the reference point everything after it improves against.

Top failing controls

Below the score sits a short table: the five CIS controls failing on the most devices across your estate, ranked by how many devices they affect (not by severity alone: a medium-severity setting that's wrong on nearly every device usually matters more day to day than a critical one wrong on a single machine). Each row shows:

  • What the control is, in plain English (e.g. “FileVault Encryption”, not a rule code)
  • How many devices are affected, as a count and a percentage of your estate
  • A severity badge: Critical, High, Medium or Low
  • One sentence on how to fix it

The email's “three things to act on” draws on this table plus the CVE correlations and configuration gaps below, picked for whichever issues clear the most findings across your estate: if you only read one part of the report, read that. For the general remediation steps behind the most common findings, see the remediation guide.

What the PDF adds

The email stays aggregate, a summary rather than a spreadsheet. The attached PDF goes further:

  • CVE correlations: software and version evidence linked to known vulnerabilities, with severity and available match confidence.
  • Operating-system currency: current and pending update evidence, its collection time, and devices too stale for a confident conclusion.
  • Recommendations: a short numbered list of the highest-impact actions, ranked by how many findings each one clears.
  • Per-device appendix: one row per device: hostname, OS, its own score, and its top failing controls. This is the page to hand to whoever does the actual fixing.

What a typical action looks like

Most findings resolve to one of two things: turn a setting on, or update a piece of software. The report doesn't just tell you something's wrong; each entry comes with the fix attached, so nobody has to go and research CIS Level 1 to act on it. If your fix touches every device the same way (for example, enabling automatic updates), it's usually worth doing it estate-wide through your IT contact or MDM rather than device by device.
